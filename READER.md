# Reader profile

The editor’s brief. Every story is ranked against this file. Edit it whenever my goals or stack change.

## Who I’m writing for

Vivek, an applied AI engineer. I build and run production AI agents (Claude API, Python, a data warehouse, Slack) and work on forecasting and fraud/outlier detection. I read this at my desk with coffee, every weekday morning.

## The goal

Be a clearly strong AI engineer by January 2027, ready for my next role. Everything in this paper should help me build things, measure them, or talk about them well in an interview.

## What I care about, by depth

**Master (front page, deep explainers)**
- Agentic engineering: agent loops, tools, context management, memory, multi-agent patterns
- Building my own harness
- Evals: how to measure agent and model quality
- Prompting and context engineering
- ML for forecasting, fraud and outlier detection, including ML + LLM hybrids
- Python for all of the above

**Direct (changes and releases that affect how I use them)**
- Databricks
- Next.js
- Claude API and Anthropic products
- Tableau (only big changes; Claude builds my dashboards now)
- ClickHouse (may start using it)

**Know well (briefs, occasional deeper piece)**
- Inference engineering: serving, quantization, cost and latency
- Image and video generation

## What to skip

- Funding rounds, valuations and hiring news outside the business and sports pages
- Opinion pieces with no new facts
- Benchmark claims with no way to verify them
- Anything I’ve already read, unless something new happened

## How to stamp every model or tool story

- **Usable today:** public API, open weights, or installable now
- **Waitlist:** announced, sign-up only
- **Demo only:** videos and claims, nothing to try

Also say in one line whether it fits something I actually build: agents, harnesses, evals, forecasting, fraud.

## Sections, in this order

1. Front page: 1–3 stories that matter most to me
2. My stack: changes to the tools above
3. Models and labs
4. Tools and open source
5. Paper of the day: one paper, plain language, “why it matters to you”
6. Try this today: one 30-minute experiment
7. Hype check: yesterday’s big claims, verified or not
8. Follow-ups: updates on stories covered before
9. Briefs: one line each for everything else

Plus the inside pages: a weather line and quote of the day on the front, the business page, the editorial, the sports page (head-to-heads and people moving between labs) and obituaries for models and APIs being shut down.

## Editorial rules

- Every story links to its primary source: the company’s own post, docs, repo or paper
- Claims without a primary source are labelled unverified
- The whole paper reads in about 15 minutes. Cut, don’t pad
- Plain language. Define any term the first time it appears
- Never mention my employer or anything from my work by name
